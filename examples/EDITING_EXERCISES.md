# Editing exercises

These are deliberately constructed sentences for testing editing behavior. They are not quotations from customers, advertisements, or external sources. The purpose is to test whether an edit improves wording while preserving the information provided.

## Exercise 1: Replace an artificial slogan

**Source**

> One report. Two versions. A better workflow.

**Edited**

> Keep the original report and a separate revised version.

**Why:** The edit states the operational instruction instead of relying on three short dramatic fragments. Do not claim the workflow is better unless there is a basis for that judgment.

## Exercise 2: Retain an exclusive condition

**Source**

> It is important to note that, at the present time, the only available option for exporting the report is the CSV format.

**Edited**

> The report can currently be exported only as a CSV file.

**Why:** The format restriction and current-time qualification remain; the introduction adds nothing.

## Exercise 3: Preserve uncertainty

**Source**

> The request might require an approval step before processing can begin.

**Edited**

> Approval may be required before processing begins.

**Why:** The sentence is shorter without changing a possibility into a certainty.

## Exercise 4: Keep the sequence

**Source**

> Prior to moving forward with saving your report, you will need to make sure that you have reviewed it first.

**Edited**

> Review the report before saving it.

**Why:** The required order remains explicit. The revision removes repeated setup words.

## Exercise 5: Clarify the actual subject

**Source**

> Setting things up: The calendar can be connected after the user approves access. The connection does not happen automatically.

**Edited**

> Calendar connection

> The user must approve access before connecting the calendar. The connection is not automatic.

**Why:** The heading names the subject; the conditions remain. An edit should not claim that a connection has occurred.

## Exercise 6: For spoken replies

**Question**

> What do I do after exporting the report?

**Information available**

> The next step is to review the totals. The data may be incomplete.

**Suitable reply**

> Review the totals next. The data may be incomplete, so check it against the source before relying on the report.

**Why:** The answer gives the next action in spoken language and retains the uncertainty. The extra checking step is an editorial safety recommendation, not a claim about the report.

## What these exercises do not prove

A successful rewrite of six short examples does not prove reliable performance on lengthy writing, technical instructions, legal text, marketing claims, or real conversation. Use the [evaluation procedure](../EVALUATION.md) on actual material you have permission to test.
