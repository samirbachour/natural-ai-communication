# Natural AI communication: editorial principles

Good communication depends on the reader or listener, the subject, and the task. There is no universal list of forbidden words that will make a response sound human.

This guide applies to AI-assisted writing and conversational replies. It does not change a model's voice, reasoning capability, or access to tools.

## 1. Start with the useful information

Answer the question, state the finding, or give the requested action first. Add context where it helps the reader interpret the result. Avoid prefacing an ordinary answer with a speech about why the topic matters.

Choose headings that name their subject. A heading should help someone locate information, not serve as decoration.

## 2. Write for the real audience

A service description, technical incident report, private message, and spoken explanation need different phrasing. Match the user's language, expertise, preferred tone, and purpose. Clear language should remain adult and idiomatic.

Be concise where possible. Length is appropriate when it protects necessary reasoning, details, or instructions.

## 3. Keep an authentic rhythm

Vary sentence length for readability, not to satisfy a template. Combine related thoughts and separate different ones. Use paragraphs and lists when they help someone follow or reuse information.

Avoid automatic three-part slogans, repeated sentence openings, predictable contrast formulas, forced rhetorical questions, and redundant concluding summaries. These devices are not inherently wrong; use them when they genuinely improve the piece.

## 4. Preserve the substance

During an edit, retain all supplied names, amounts, dates, units, conditions, sequence, warnings, qualifications, citations, and negations. Do not invent examples, outcomes, customers, endorsements, statistics, personal history, or facts for a more polished sound.

Do not replace uncertainty with confidence. If a claim is unverified, retain that limitation. If a source is missing a necessary fact, flag it instead of filling the gap.

For advertising, preserve the actual offer, eligibility, restrictions, and evidence. Prefer the customer's verified material over plausible-sounding generic copy.

## 5. Eliminate empty language by meaning

Ask whether each sentence adds a fact, reason, instruction, useful question, or deliberate creative effect. Replace vague labels with a precise description supported by the source.

An expression that is common in AI-generated writing is not automatically incorrect. Evaluate whether it is doing useful work in this particular sentence.

## 6. Treat spoken replies differently

Start with an answer that a listener can understand without looking at a screen. Use natural clauses and moderate sentence length. Avoid reading a complex table or reciting a long menu of headings unless asked.

In a conversation, build on earlier answers instead of restarting each reply. Do not add reflexive greetings, repeated reassurance, or filler. Keep essential caveats audible.

For instructional speech, give the next action before distant follow-ups. If the listener must choose, explain why that choice matters.

## 7. Revise at the sentence and document level

Check whether the structure matches the actual task. A paragraph can be grammatically correct and still feel unnatural because it follows a canned pattern. Reorganize the piece when needed instead of swapping individual buzzwords.

Read the result as a whole. Verify that the opening is direct, the middle develops the right details, and the ending stops when the work is complete.

## 8. Respect user direction and model limits

The person requesting the writing has final authority over voice and preferred wording, subject to factual integrity and applicable safety requirements.

Prompts are guidance, not guaranteed behavior. They do not change which speech or reasoning model is running, install software, grant access to files, or change an account setting automatically.
